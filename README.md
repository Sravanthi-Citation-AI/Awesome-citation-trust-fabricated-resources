## Overview

Generative AI can produce research content with citations that appear scholarly and credible. However, a citation can contain incorrect metadata, point to the wrong source, or refer to a publication that does not exist.

This repository focuses on the relationship between citation formatting, source credibility, and human trust in fabricated or AI-generated resources. It also considers the importance of verifying citations rather than assuming that professional-looking references are genuine.

The project is based on the research topic:

**The Influence of Citation Formatting on Human Trust in Fabricated Resources**

The work examines citation credibility, authenticity, source verification, AI-generated references, and the potential risks of trusting apparently credible citations.

## Citation Integrity Audit

The citation audit examines whether references generated in the research paper actually exist and whether their bibliographic information is accurate.

The audit checks:

- Publication existence
- Title accuracy
- Author accuracy
- Publication year
- Journal or conference
- DOI, PMID, or arXiv identifier
- Connection between citations and research claims

In the completed audit, 10 references were examined. 9 were verified and 1 was classified as fabricated. The resulting authenticity score was **90/100**, with a prediction accuracy of **90%**.

## Research Papers

### Foundational Research

- **The Influence of Source Credibility on Communication Effectiveness**  
  Hovland, C. I., & Weiss, W. (1951). *Public Opinion Quarterly*.  
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/266350)  
  Foundational work on how source credibility influences communication.

- **The Elements of Computer Credibility**  
  Fogg, B. J., & Tseng, H. (1999). *CHI Conference Proceedings*.  
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/302979.303001)  
  Provides a foundation for understanding credibility in computer-mediated information.

### Citation and AI Research

- **Fabrication and errors in the bibliographic citations generated by ChatGPT**  
  Walters, W. H., & Wilder, E. I. (2023). *Scientific Reports, 13*, 14045.  
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-41032-5)  
  Examines fabricated bibliographic references generated by ChatGPT.

- **Citations and Trust in LLM Generated Responses**  
  Ding, Y., et al. (2025). *Proceedings of the AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence*.  
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v39i22.34550)  
  Investigates the relationship between citations and human trust in LLM-generated responses.

- **The influence of source cues and topic familiarity on credibility evaluation**  
  Lucassen, T., & Schraagen, J. M. (2013). *Computers in Human Behavior, 29*(4), 1387–1392.  
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2013.01.036)  
  Examines how source cues and familiarity affect credibility evaluation.

- **Source Cues in Online News: Is the Proximate Source More Powerful than Distal Sources?**  
  Kang, H., Bae, K., Zhang, S., & Sundar, S. S. (2011). *Journalism & Mass Communication Quarterly, 88*(4), 719–736.  
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/107769901108800403)  
  Relevant to understanding how source cues affect perceived credibility.

- **Citation Errors in Scientific Research and Publications: Causes, Consequences, and Remedies**  
  Agarwal, A., et al. (2023). *The World Journal of Men's Health, 41*(3), 461–465.  
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5534/wjmh.230001)  
  Discusses citation errors and their consequences for scholarly communication.

## Key Findings

The citation audit demonstrated that professional-looking citations cannot automatically be assumed to be genuine. In the completed audit, 9 of 10 audited references were verified, while 1 reference was classified as fabricated.

The audit produced:

- **Authenticity Score:** 90/100
- **Prediction Accuracy:** 90%
- **Verified references:** 9/10
- **Fabricated references:** 1/10

The main lesson is that citation appearance alone is not sufficient evidence of authenticity. Citations should be checked against reliable scholarly or publisher records.
