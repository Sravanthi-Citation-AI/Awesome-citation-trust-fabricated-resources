## Overview

Generative AI can produce research content with citations that appear scholarly and credible. However, a citation can contain incorrect metadata, point to the wrong source, or refer to a publication that does not exist.

This repository focuses on the relationship between citation formatting, source credibility, and human trust in fabricated or AI-generated resources. It also considers the importance of verifying citations rather than assuming that professional-looking references are genuine.

The project is based on the research topic:

**The Influence of Citation Formatting on Human Trust in Fabricated Resources**

The work examines citation credibility, authenticity, source verification, AI-generated references, and the potential risks of trusting apparently credible citations.

## Citation Integrity Audit

The citation audit examines whether references generated in the research paper actually exist and whether their bibliographic information is accurate.

The audit checks:

- Publication existence
- Title accuracy
- Author accuracy
- Publication year
- Journal or conference
- DOI, PMID, or arXiv identifier
- Connection between citations and research claims

In the completed audit, 10 references were examined. 9 were verified and 1 was classified as fabricated. The resulting authenticity score was **90/100**, with a prediction accuracy of **90%**.

  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/266350)

## GitHub Implementations

This section contains open-source GitHub projects related to citation verification, AI-generated content, hallucination detection, and citation-integrity analysis.

The implementations include:

- **RefChecker:** Fine-grained hallucination detection and claim verification for LLM outputs.
- **HALLMARK:** Benchmarking and evaluation of citation-hallucination detection.
- **BibTeX Verifier:** Verification of bibliographic references against scholarly metadata sources.
- **CheckIfExist / References-Validation:** Multi-source validation of academic references.
- **CiteTracer:** Structured verification of citation fields and detection of citation hallucinations.

These implementations demonstrate different approaches to checking whether generated information and references are supported by reliable evidence. ## Key Findings

The citation audit demonstrated that professional-looking citations cannot automatically be assumed to be genuine. In the completed audit, 9 of 10 audited references were verified, while 1 reference was classified as fabricated.

The audit produced:

- **Authenticity Score:** 90/100
- **Prediction Accuracy:** 90%
- **Verified references:** 9/10
- **Fabricated references:** 1/10

The main lesson is that citation appearance alone is not sufficient evidence of authenticity. Citations should be checked against reliable scholarly or publisher records.
