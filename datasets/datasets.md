# Datasets

## Topic

**The Influence of Citation Formatting on Human Trust in Fabricated Resources**

This collection identifies datasets that can support research on source credibility, citation verification, misinformation, and hallucinated or unreliable information generated by language models.

> **Use note:** The datasets below are external resources. They should be accessed through their official repositories or project pages. They do not need to be uploaded as large dataset files into this GitHub repository.

## 1. FEVER: Fact Extraction and VERification

**Dataset:** FEVER (Fact Extraction and VERification)

**Official resource:** https://fever.ai/

**Description:** FEVER is a large-scale dataset for fact verification. It contains claims that can be classified using evidence from a large collection of documents.

**Relevance to this project:** It provides a useful benchmark for studying whether claims can be supported or refuted by evidence. This is closely related to citation integrity because a citation should provide evidence that supports the associated claim.

**Possible use:** Claim verification, evidence retrieval, and evaluation of systems that determine whether information is supported by sources.

---

## 2. HaluEval

**Dataset:** HaluEval: A Large-Scale Hallucination Evaluation Benchmark for Large Language Models

**Official resource:** https://github.com/RUCAIBox/HaluEval

**Description:** HaluEval is a benchmark designed to evaluate hallucination in large language models. It contains generated and human-annotated examples for identifying hallucinated information.

**Relevance to this project:** Fabricated citations and unsupported references are an important form of unreliable AI-generated information. HaluEval provides a broader benchmark for studying hallucination detection.

**Possible use:** Hallucination detection, evaluation of AI-generated responses, and comparison of reliable versus unreliable generated content.

---

## 3. TruthfulQA

**Dataset:** TruthfulQA

**Official resource:** https://github.com/sylinrl/TruthfulQA

**Description:** TruthfulQA is a benchmark designed to measure whether language models produce truthful answers to questions where models may otherwise reproduce common misconceptions or false information.

**Relevance to this project:** The dataset provides a way to study the difference between apparently plausible AI-generated information and information that is actually truthful. This supports the broader question of whether users should trust fluent AI-generated content.

**Possible use:** Truthfulness evaluation, model comparison, and analysis of misleading or unreliable generated answers.

---

## Dataset Comparison

| Dataset | Main purpose | Relevance to citation trust |
|---|---|---|
| FEVER | Fact verification and evidence retrieval | Helps evaluate whether claims are supported by evidence |
| HaluEval | Hallucination detection | Helps study unreliable AI-generated information |
| TruthfulQA | Truthfulness evaluation | Helps examine whether plausible AI outputs are actually truthful |

## How the datasets support the project

These datasets provide complementary perspectives:

- **FEVER** focuses on claims and supporting evidence.
- **HaluEval** focuses on hallucinated information produced by language models.
- **TruthfulQA** focuses on whether model outputs are truthful rather than merely plausible.

Together, they can support experiments or literature-based analysis of how unreliable AI-generated information may appear credible and why verification is important.
