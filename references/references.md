# References

## Topic

**The Influence of Citation Formatting on Human Trust in Fabricated Resources**

This reference collection supports the research topic by covering source credibility, online information credibility, trust, verification behavior, citation integrity, and fabricated or hallucinated references produced by generative AI.

> **Verification note:** The references below were checked against publisher, DOI, PubMed, or institutional records while preparing this file. Links are provided to authoritative records where available. No copyrighted article PDFs are included in this repository.

## A. Source Credibility and Human Trust

1. **Hovland, C. I., & Weiss, W. (1951).** The influence of source credibility on communication effectiveness. *Public Opinion Quarterly, 15*(4), 635–650.  
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1086/266350  
   **Relevance:** Foundational research on how source credibility affects acceptance of information.

2. **Fogg, B. J., & Tseng, H. (1999).** The elements of computer credibility. *Proceedings of the SIGCHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems*, 80–87.  
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/302979.303001  
   **Relevance:** Establishes concepts for understanding credibility in computer-mediated information.

3. **Flanagin, A. J., & Metzger, M. J. (2000).** Perceptions of Internet information credibility. *Journalism & Mass Communication Quarterly, 77*(3), 515–540.  
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/107769900007700304  
   **Relevance:** Examines how people perceive the credibility of Internet information and whether they verify it.

4. **Metzger, M. J., Flanagin, A. J., & Zwarun, L. (2003).** College student Web use, perceptions of information credibility, and verification behavior. *Computers & Education, 41*(3), 271–290.  
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0360-1315(03)00049-6  
   **Relevance:** Directly connects perceived credibility with verification behavior.

5. **Fogg, B. J., Soohoo, C., Danielson, D. R., Marable, L., Stanford, J., & Tauber, E. R. (2003).** How do users evaluate the credibility of Web sites? A study with over 2,500 participants. *Proceedings of the 2003 Conference on Designing for User Experiences*.  
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/997078.997097  
   **Relevance:** Large participant study of the features people notice when judging Web credibility.

6. **Metzger, M. J. (2007).** Making sense of credibility on the Web: Models for evaluating online information and recommendations for future research. *Journal of the American Society for Information Science and Technology, 58*(13), 2078–2091.  
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1002/asi.20672  
   **Relevance:** Reviews models and empirical research on evaluating online information credibility.

7. **Flanagin, A. J., & Metzger, M. J. (2007).** The role of site features, user attributes, and information verification behaviors on the perceived credibility of Web-based information. *New Media & Society, 9*(2), 319–342.  
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/1461444807075015  
   **Relevance:** Studies how site features and verification behavior influence perceived credibility.

8. **Kang, H., Bae, K., Zhang, S., & Sundar, S. S. (2011).** Source cues in online news: Is the proximate source more powerful than distal sources? *Journalism & Mass Communication Quarterly, 88*(4), 719–736.  
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/107769901108800403  
   **Relevance:** Examines how source cues influence perceived credibility in online information.

9. **Lucassen, T., & Schraagen, J. M. (2013).** The influence of source cues and topic familiarity on credibility evaluation. *Computers in Human Behavior, 29*(4), 1387–1392.  
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2013.01.036  
   **Relevance:** Studies how source cues and topic familiarity affect trust in information.

10. **Metzger, M. J., & Flanagin, A. J. (2013).** Credibility and trust of information in online environments: The use of cognitive heuristics. *Journal of Pragmatics, 59*, 210–220.  
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pragma.2013.07.012  
    **Relevance:** Explains how people use cognitive shortcuts when judging online information credibility.

11. **Flanagin, A. J., Winter, S., & Metzger, M. J. (2020).** Making sense of credibility in complex information environments: The role of message sidedness, information source, and thinking styles in credibility evaluation online. *Information, Communication & Society, 23*(7), 1038–1056.  
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/1369118X.2018.1547411  
    **Relevance:** Examines source and message factors that shape credibility judgments.

12. **Sbaffi, L., & Rowley, J. (2017).** Trust and credibility in Web-based health information: A review and agenda for future research. *Journal of Medical Internet Research, 19*(6), e218.  
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.2196/jmir.7579  
    **Relevance:** Systematic review of factors affecting online trust and credibility.

13. **Lee, C., Kim, J., & Chan-Olmsted, S. M. (2010).** Branded product information search on the Web: The role of brand trust and credibility of online information sources. *Journal of Marketing Communications, 17*(5), 355–374.  
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/13527266.2010.484128  
    **Relevance:** Connects source credibility and trust with online information seeking.

## B. Citation Integrity and Fabricated References

14. **Walters, W. H., & Wilder, E. I. (2023).** Fabrication and errors in the bibliographic citations generated by ChatGPT. *Scientific Reports, 13*, 14045.  
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-41032-5  
    **Relevance:** Directly investigates fabricated and erroneous bibliographic citations generated by ChatGPT.

15. **Day, T. (2023).** A preliminary investigation of fake peer-reviewed citations and references generated by ChatGPT. *The Professional Geographer, 75*(6), 1024–1027.  
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/00330124.2023.2190373  
    **Relevance:** Investigates fake peer-reviewed citations produced by ChatGPT.

16. **Giray, L. (2024).** ChatGPT references unveiled: Distinguishing the reliable from the fake. *Internet Reference Services Quarterly, 28*(1), 9–18.  
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/10875301.2023.2265369  
    **Relevance:** Examines the reliability of ChatGPT-generated references across source types.

17. **McGowan, A., Gui, Y., Dobbs, M., Shuster, S., Cotter, M., Selloni, A., Goodman, M., Srivastava, A., Cecchi, G. A., & Corcoran, C. M. (2023).** ChatGPT and Bard exhibit spontaneous citation fabrication during psychiatry literature search. *Psychiatry Research, 329*, 115334.  
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psychres.2023.115334  
    **Relevance:** Reports citation fabrication by generative AI systems during literature searches.

18. **Ding, Y., Facciani, M., Joyce, E., Poudel, A., Bhattacharya, S., Veeramani, B., Aguinaga, S., & Weninger, T. (2025).** Citations and trust in LLM generated responses. *Proceedings of the AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence, 39*(22), 23787–23795.  
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v39i22.34550  
    **Relevance:** Directly studies how the presence and checking of citations affect trust in LLM-generated responses.

19. **Picazo-Sanchez, P., & Ortiz-Martin, L. (2026).** Evaluating the integrity of LLM-generated citations: Prevalence and risks of fabricated references in scientific literature. *Data, 11*(5), 122.  
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/data11050122  
    **Relevance:** Quantifies and analyzes fabricated references generated by multiple LLMs and discusses scholarly integrity risks.

20. **Resnik, D. B., & Hosseini, M. (2026).** Hallucinated citations produced by generative artificial intelligence may constitute research misconduct when citations function as data in scholarly papers.  
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/08989621.2026.2645390  
    **Relevance:** Discusses the research-integrity implications of hallucinated citations when citations are part of scholarly evidence.

21. **Davis, K., & Mahmoud, Q. (2026).** Citation constraints and reference hallucinations in large language models. *Proceedings of the 39th Canadian Conference on Artificial Intelligence*, PMLR 318, 464–475.  
    DOI/Record: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v318/davis26a.html  
    **Relevance:** Studies reference hallucinations under different citation and prompting constraints.

22. **Spennemann, D. H. R. (2026).** How unique are hallucinated citations offered by generative artificial intelligence models? *Publications, 14*(3), 38.  
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/publications14030038  
    **Relevance:** Examines recurring hallucinated academic references and the propagation of fabricated citations.

23. **Agarwal, A., Arafa, M., Avidor-Reiss, T., et al. (2023).** Citation errors in scientific research and publications: Causes, consequences, and remedies. *The World Journal of Men's Health, 41*(3), 461–465.  
    DOI: https://doi.org/10.5534/wjmh.230001  
    **Relevance:** Reviews citation errors, their causes, consequences, and ways to improve citation accuracy.

## How these references support the project

The collection is organized around three connected ideas:

1. **Credibility and trust:** foundational research explains how people judge sources and information.
2. **Citation verification:** research on online credibility and verification explains why readers may accept information without checking it.
3. **AI-generated citation integrity:** recent studies document fabricated references, citation errors, and the effect of citations on trust in LLM-generated responses.

These themes directly support the project topic, **The Influence of Citation Formatting on Human Trust in Fabricated Resources**, and provide a research base for the repository's citation-integrity audit.
